Exchange Rate Margin Explained

When sending money internationally, many people focus on the visible transfer fee. But in many cases, the biggest cost is hidden in the exchange rate itself. This hidden cost is called the exchange rate margin.

What is an exchange rate margin?

The exchange rate margin is the difference between the real (mid-market) exchange rate and the rate offered by a bank or transfer service. Instead of charging a large fee upfront, some providers earn money by offering a worse exchange rate.

Why it often costs more than the transfer fee

Even a small margin can become expensive on larger transfers. For example, a 1% margin on a $1,000 transfer is effectively a $10 cost — often higher than the “fee” shown on the screen.

How to estimate the real cost

The simplest way to estimate the true cost is to compare two outcomes:

  • How much the sender pays (including any fees)
  • How much the recipient receives after conversion

The difference between what you pay and what the recipient receives (converted at the real exchange rate) represents the true total cost — and the exchange rate margin is often a major part of it.

Why this matters for comparisons

When comparing banks and fintech services, do not compare fees alone. Compare the final received amount for the same send amount (or the required send amount for the same receive amount). This approach makes hidden exchange rate costs visible.
